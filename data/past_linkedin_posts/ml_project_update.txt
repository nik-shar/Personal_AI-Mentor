Deployed my first end-to-end ML project to production last month. The model wasn't the hardest part.

Training a model that works in a notebook is one skill. Getting it to serve real requests reliably — with sane latency, proper error handling, and a way to update it without downtime — is a completely different one.

The things that slowed me down most had nothing to do with ML: dependency management, environment parity between dev and prod, logging that actually helps you debug at 2am.

I'm a Civil Engineering student. None of this was in my curriculum. Everything I know about production AI systems came from building things, breaking them, and reading a lot of GitHub issues.

If you're studying something unrelated to CS and trying to break into AI engineering — the gap is closeable. It just takes longer than the bootcamp ads suggest.
